Ruling (Terje, 2026-08-06, SB-026 probe review; **amended same day by SB-040**): the editor is a hybrid of two surfaces over one store. The script/document surface is where authoring starts — write the document, see what facts it lifts. The canvas is where you wire and navigate — connect facts to the dialogs they trigger, follow structure, get the overview. Two lenses on the same markup, jump-to-block both ways.

Amendment history: the original SB-026 ruling made the canvas THE editor. The SB-036 probe falsified that form ("a form bolted on a diagram") — the real authoring loop is linear and document-first. The SB-038 survey showed the same consensus in Twine, Yarn Spinner, Ink, and Blueprints: text and graph are two lenses on one file, and no successful tool births prose on the graph. SB-040 rules: neither surface is "primary" — each owns the gestures it is best at.

What stays from the original ruling: content/cases/*.case.md remains the single source of truth. Every edit on either surface compiles to a markup patch + recompile. Neither surface is a second format. Content stays git-diffable; the 0.2 compiler contract holds. The canvas is NOT demoted to read-only — wiring on it worked in the probes and stays.

Trade-off accepted: two editable surfaces cost more than one (both need write-back, cross-jump, and shared diagnostics). A single-surface editor would have been cheaper — and each single-surface form has now failed a probe (script-only in SB-025's note, canvas-only in SB-036).
