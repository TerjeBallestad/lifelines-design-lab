Terje's idea from the SB-106 grill: Frank holds a priority-ordered list of goals per case ("get to know Elling"), and when a goal is denied (Elling refuses to talk), Frank falls back to a secondary goal (explore the apartment, talk to Grete). Same shape on the phone: call Elling, Grete answers, renegotiate. Possibly a player-facing priority screen (RimWorld work-priorities analogy) where the player orders goals/missions in the case.

This is the director tier ABOVE the stage/role/duty scene model (SB-105's two-tier lesson: premise and pressure above, choreography below). The SB-106 vocabulary survives untouched: "Elling refuses" is a fail trigger on a stage. Today every fail routes to the call-off (TERM-006). This ticket asks whether a fail can instead route to another goal's stage list, how denial is detected, how goals are authored, and what the player controls.

The questions: goal as an authorable unit (relation to Oppdrag?), denial detection, fallback routing vs call-off, the priority screen (is it fun or micromanagement?), scoring.
